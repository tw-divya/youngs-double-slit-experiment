Theory and Learning

1. Wave nature of light

Light is an electromagnetic wave. Visible wavelengths span about 400 nm (violet) to 700 nm (red). Interference and diffraction confirm this wave behaviour.

2. Superposition

When two waves overlap, the resultant displacement is the sum of the individual displacements.

3. Coherent sources

Sources with the same frequency and a constant phase difference. In YDSE, one source illuminates both slits, so S1 and S2 are coherent.

4. Constructive interference

Waves arrive in phase: Δ = mλ → bright fringe, maximum intensity.

5. Destructive interference

Waves arrive out of phase: Δ = (m+½)λ → dark fringe, minimum intensity.

6. Path difference

Δ = d sinθ ≈ d·y/D for small θ (far field, D ≫ d).

7. Fringe width

β = λD/d. Larger λ or D widens fringes; larger d narrows them. Bright positions: yₘ = mβ; dark: (m+½)β.

8. Young's double-slit experiment

Monochromatic light passes through two narrow slits; the screen shows alternating bright and dark fringes with I = Imax cos²(πdy/λD).

9. Applications

  • Measuring wavelength
  • Thin-film coatings and anti-reflective lenses
  • Interferometers (Michelson, LIGO)
  • Fibre-optic sensors, holography

Model assumptions

Ideal coherent slits of equal intensity, far-field and small-angle approximation. The finite-slit-width diffraction envelope is not included.
